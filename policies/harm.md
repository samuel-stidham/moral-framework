# The Harm Boundary - Version 3.0.0

## Purpose

This policy states the duties that bar harm, the license to respond to danger, and the repair a response can owe. Its core conditions are explicit, and judgment covers the rest. It is written in a neutral voice so any framework may adopt it by reference. It is modular. It stands alone except for the delegated terms listed under Dependencies. It states its floor on persons, and the acts no institution may commit. Those it does not delegate. Its evaluative standard is the reasonable person, named wherever it does work.

## The Policy in Brief

Do not intentionally or carelessly harm another's body, mind, property, or rightful liberty. Valid consent, licensed response to live danger, and qualifying institutional action are the only permissions. An institution qualifies only when it is lawful, uses fair process, and stays within the human rights floor. A response must be necessary and proportionate, and it ends when the danger ends. Never torture. No institution may put a person to death as punishment. Whoever stages a danger or carelessly creates one bears its foreseeable costs. Apply every rule in good faith. This brief states the policy's controlling spirit. The detailed rules guide its application. No technical reading overrides the refusal to inflict unnecessary harm.

## The Two Tracks

The policy answers two different questions, and every clause belongs to exactly one.

The **violation track** asks whether someone broke the duty.

The **license track** asks whether a response was permitted.

One index rule governs both tracks. Duty and license verdicts on a person's act use that person's position. That position holds the facts, the actor's intent or carelessness, and what a reasonable person there would perceive. Other people's acts, words, expectations, and consents enter that judgment as facts. No one borrows a reasonableness standard from another person's position. A definition that names its own position overrides this default. Degree terms, such as grave, minor, credible, and substantial, are judged by the reasonable person in the acting person's position. Rule V3 is the exception. Every term in that rule is judged by a reasonable person outside the institution.

The tracks can diverge. A licensed response can coexist with no violation by anyone. When two people act on reasonable misperception, the tragedy has victims and no violator.

## Definitions

**Person.** Every human being is a person. An adopting framework may recognize more, and never fewer. No account of this term may exclude any human being, on any ground. Those grounds include race, color, sex, language, religion, opinion, origin, property, birth, and other status. This draws on the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Articles 1, 2, and 6 (United Nations, 1948).

**Harm.** A setback to a person's body, mind, property, or rightful liberty. Harm to the mind means inflicted psychological injury. Hurt feelings alone do not qualify. Conveying information in good faith, believed true on reasonable grounds, is not infliction of psychological injury. Offense, disagreement, and losing a fair contest freely entered are not harm.

**Intent.** Acting with the aim of causing harm, or knowing harm is practically certain to follow.

**Careless risk.** Risk a reasonable person would not impose, given the activity's value and the precautions available.

**Consent.** Permission for a harm, given by the competent person who would bear it. Valid consent is informed, freely given, and revocable until the act passes its point of no return. Consent valid at that point covers what follows. Permission obtained by deception or coercion is not consent. A competent person's free, informed choice to bear a harm counts as valid consent to it. No one can consent to harm borne by someone else, with one exception. A guardian the adopting framework recognizes may consent for a person lacking competence, in that person's interest. Where consent cannot be obtained in time, consent is presumed. The presumption covers harm a reasonable person would accept. It must avert greater harm to the person who would bear it. Consent is never presumed against a competent refusal the actor knows of or should know of. Deliberately avoiding that knowledge counts as knowing. A guardian's refusal binds only within the person's interest. It never defeats presumed consent for harm needed to avert grave harm to that person. A guardian's refusal outside the person's interest leaves consent unobtainable, so the presumption may still operate.

**Permitted harm.** Harm within valid consent, harm within a licensed response under the Rules, or institutional harm meeting Rule V3's conditions. A killing Rule V1 bars is never permitted harm. All other harm is unpermitted. Unpermitted marks the absence of a permission, never a verdict on whoever caused the harm. Blameless accident is unpermitted and violates no duty. Rule V1 states what the duty forbids.

**Attempt.** Taking a substantial step toward inflicting harm the agent intends, knowing or having reason to know the facts that make it unpermitted.

**Threat.** Intentionally or recklessly leading another to expect unpermitted harm from the threatener, or from forces the threatener directs or controls. A threat can travel through words, conduct, or both. Anger, hyperbole, and jokes are no threat when a reasonable person in the recipient's position would find no serious commitment behind them.

**Danger.** A danger exists for a responder when a reasonable person in the responder's position would perceive qualifying harm. Qualifying harm is imminent, in progress, or reversible on the spot. It befalls at least one person, whether or not yet identified, the endangered. The endangered may be the responder or another. It is not within valid consent. Institutional harm qualifies only where the responder reasonably perceives a breach of Rule V3's floor. Failing Rule V3's other conditions may breach the duty. It licenses nothing. Harm Rule V1's killing bar forbids stays qualifying, whatever the responder perceives. Harm the responder knows or should know lies within a licensed response is not qualifying harm. Harm to the responder's own body or mind stays qualifying even then. The source's state of mind does not matter. Intent, carelessness, accident, and innocent conduct can each pose a danger. Attempted concealment does not defeat a danger the responder can perceive. A danger is live while the harm can still be averted or undone on the spot.

**Source.** The person or object whose presence, condition, conduct, or motion carries or sustains a danger.

**Torture.** Deliberately inflicting severe suffering to break a person's will.

## Rules

The violation rules judge acts. The license rules judge responses. The index rule governs both.

### Rule V1: The duty

Do not inflict on another person harm you intend, knowing or having reason to know the facts that make it unpermitted. Inflicting includes acting through an instrument, an innocent agent, or a person one coerces. Do not attempt such harm. Do not threaten harm, knowing or having reason to know the facts that make the threatened harm unpermitted. Do not impose careless risk of harm on others. Do not torture. No institution may put a person to death as punishment, however grave the offense and however regular the process. No consent makes a punishment something else. The bar also reaches any other institutional killing done outside a live danger, unless it falls within valid consent. Neither consent, nor license, nor institutional process permits torture or the threat of it. These duties bind everyone, always, with only the boundaries these rules state.

### Rule V2: Consent

Harm within valid consent violates no duty, except torture, which nothing permits. Surgery and sport live here.

### Rule V3: Institutions

Harm imposed by an institution violates no duty when three conditions hold. The institution is lawful. It acted through fair process. The harm stayed within the floor.

Fair process means the institution's own lawful procedures. Before any punishment that deprives a person of liberty, it also means a public hearing before an independent and impartial tribunal. A competent person may waive that hearing. It means notice of the charge, a real chance to answer it, and the presumption of innocence. Those come from Articles 10 and 11 (United Nations, 1948).

The floor bars ten things, whatever an institution's own law says. No institution may hold a person in slavery or servitude. Work law requires of everyone alike, or a lawful sentence requires, is neither. A sentence of forced labor is not covered. No institution may subject a person to torture, or to cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment. The ordinary incidents of lawful custody are not degrading. No institution may arbitrarily arrest, detain, or exile a person. No institution may deny a person recognition as a person before the law. No institution may arbitrarily deny a person equal protection of the law. No institution may arbitrarily treat a person worse because of race, color, sex, language, religion, opinion, origin, property, birth, or other status. No institution may punish a person under a law that did not exist when the act or omission occurred. Nor may it impose a heavier penalty than the law then allowed. No institution may punish a person for a belief, for expressing one, for practicing a religion, or for seeking or receiving information. A threat, a deception, or a call to violence is none of those. No institution may withhold available food, water, or medical care from a person in its custody. Care validly refused is not withheld. This bar alone yields to valid consent. No institution may arbitrarily strip a person of what their own life is built on. That reaches their liberty, home, privacy, family, work, property, nationality, and voice in public life.

These bars draw on the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (United Nations, 1948). They come from Articles 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11, 18, 19, and 25. The general bar draws on Articles 12, 13, 15, 16, 17, 20, 21, and 23. The custody bar narrows Article 25 and extends it. The nine named bars are the gravest cases. The general bar carries the rest, and asks only whether the institution had a reason.

The floor is a bar on acts, never a test of the institution as a whole. No institution's own law moves it. No permission in this policy reaches harm the floor bars, except as the custody bar allows. A killing Rule V1 bars is never permitted here, even where these conditions hold.

### Rule V4: Staging

Deliberately or recklessly creating the appearance of a danger, without reasonable precautions against misperception, imposes careless risk and violates the duty. A stager in violation bears the loss any licensed response the staged appearance foreseeably provokes, directly or through further reasonable misperception.

### Rule V5: Good faith

These rules are written for good faith. Deliberately engineering a rule's conditions, to bring harm about or to excuse it, violates the duty. The letter of no rule launders a plan to hurt someone.

### Rule L1: The response license

A responder may answer a danger with harm, or with a threat of harm. The response is licensed only while all four conditions hold.

1. **Live danger.** A danger exists for the responder and is live.
2. **Necessity.** No course inflicting less harm and open to the responder averts the danger. If an exit avoids all threatened harm, take the exit.
3. **Proportionality.** The response may not greatly exceed the harm it prevents. Deadly force answers only credible dangers to life or limb. If every effective response is excessive, no harmful response is licensed.
4. **Clean hands.** The responder did not bring the danger about by conduct violating the duty, or by deliberate provocation. Deliberate provocation means conduct meant to draw an attack. Whether the responder brought the danger about turns only on their conduct before it arose. A danger arises anew once the responder has fully withdrawn and shown it. It also arises anew when a response that held a license exceeds it. A response that was never licensed triggers a new arising only when it greatly exceeds what the responder's conduct foreseeably invited. Conduct before a new arising no longer counts as bringing the new danger about. Clean hands never bars answering a danger the responder's own conduct created, when the response protects someone else. That responder owes repair for every harm the response inflicts.

A licensed response may fall only on the source of the danger, except as Rule L2 permits. Harm to a property interest in the source counts as falling on the source. Where a person wrongfully made the source carry the danger, that person owes repair to the innocent source or its owner. Where the property's own behavior carried the danger, and no person wrongfully made it do so, no repair is owed. Otherwise the responder owes the blameless owner repair. A response in threat form is licensed while all four conditions hold. Its condition-three measure is the harm the threat itself inflicts, never the harm it names. A response in threat form may name harm to the source alone, never to anyone else. A warning naming only what a license would permit is no threat and needs no license. A licensed threat violates no duty. Carrying it out is a fresh response, judged on its own conditions. A threat of deadly force stays non-deadly while the responder's aim stops at deterrence. Neither torture nor the threat of it is ever licensed.

### Rule L2: Bystanders

Answering or escaping a grave danger may harm a bystander's property when conditions one through four hold. It may never harm a bystander's body or mind. The property harm must stay minor beside the harm answered or escaped. The responder owes the bystander repair.

### Rule L3: Expiry

The license ends the moment the danger stops being live. Harm after that point is retaliation and violates the duty. The aftermath belongs to lawful institutions. Private payback stays forbidden.

### Rule L4: No transfer

The license attaches to the live danger alone. It never extends to grievance, group membership, or past wrongs. Defense of the endangered is direct response, never transfer. Defense of a person fails against a response licensed by that person's own duty-violating conduct. This bar binds only responders who know or should know those facts.

## Dependencies

Five terms take their content from the adopting framework: property, rightful liberty, fair contest, lawful institutions, and competence. Rule V3 defines fair process and the Definitions define person, so neither is delegated. An adopting framework may recognize more persons than the Definitions require, never fewer. Who may serve as guardian follows the competence account. This policy does not define these terms. State your account of each when you adopt it. The lawful institutions delegation carries heavy loads, including arrest and other pre-judgment coercion. Rule V3 bounds that coercion by the floor, which bars arbitrary arrest and detention. The reasonable person is the policy's own standard and is not delegated.

## Judgment and Law

This policy is a personal decision procedure. It is neither a complete moral system nor a substitute for law. It guides the reader's own conduct. It judges institutional acts without commanding institutions. It does not decide what one nation owes another. It licenses no one to go looking for a danger to answer.

It does not excuse violations of valid law, with one exception. A licensed response to a grave and live danger needs no statute's permission, and no statute removes it. Outside that exception, when valid law forbids conduct this policy permits, follow the law. When valid law permits conduct this policy forbids, follow this policy. When valid law requires conduct this policy forbids, seek lawful review, exception, or accommodation where available. Unjust law is challenged through institutional process. Private force answers only a live danger, on Rule L1's conditions. The policy cannot settle every conflict of conscience. Valid means valid under the adopting framework's foundations, never merely enacted.

Where the text is silent or indeterminate, choose the least harmful reasonable course. Interpret doubtful provisions according to the policy's spirit: avoid unnecessary harm, use force only against live danger, and repair avoidable losses.

## Amendment

Reopen this policy only when one of three things happens.

1. Two provisions give conflicting guidance on the same case.
2. A recurring real situation defeats the policy's stated spirit.
3. An ambiguity materially changes how a conscientious person would behave.

A newly constructed hypothetical reopens nothing. The reasonable person, the least-harm fallback, and the controlling spirit exist because enumeration must stop.

## Lineage

This policy is a specification of nonmaleficence, the duty not to inflict harm on others. Nonmaleficence entered modern ethics as one of W. D. Ross's prima facie duties (Skelton, 2022). The definition of harm adapts Joel Feinberg's account of harm as a setback to interests (Feinberg, 1984). The consent definition follows Feinberg's account of consent as the bearer's own normative power, and the floor takes his anti-paternalist line (Feinberg, 1986). The response conditions draw on Jeff McMahan's account of liability to defensive harm (McMahan, 2009). The separation of necessity from proportionality follows the standard analysis of self-defense (Frowe & Parry, 2021). The license against innocent sources extends Judith Jarvis Thomson's account of self-defense (Thomson, 1991). The threat definition borrows from the true threat doctrine in American law. Hyperbole is no threat (_Watts v. United States_, 1969). A speaker must intend the threat or be reckless about how it lands (_Counterman v. Colorado_, 2023). The institutional floor takes its content from the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (United Nations, 1948). The bar on judicial killing is not drawn from that Declaration, which is silent on capital punishment. The slavery bar's exception for required work is not drawn from it either. The two-track structure states the distinction between wrongdoing and permission as explicit rules.
