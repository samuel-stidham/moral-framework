# The Harm Boundary - Version 2.0.0

## Purpose

This policy states the duties that bar harm, the license to respond to danger, and the repair a response can owe. Its core conditions are explicit, and judgment covers the rest. It is written in a neutral voice so any framework may adopt it by reference. It is modular. It stands alone except for the delegated terms listed under Dependencies. Its evaluative standard is the reasonable person, named wherever it does work.

## The Policy in Brief

Do not intentionally or carelessly harm another's body, mind, property, or rightful liberty. Valid consent, licensed response to live danger, and lawful institutions acting fairly are the only permissions. A response must be necessary and proportionate, and it ends when the danger ends. Never torture. Whoever stages a danger or carelessly creates one bears its foreseeable costs. Apply every rule in good faith. This brief states the policy's controlling spirit. The detailed rules guide its application. No technical reading overrides the refusal to inflict unnecessary harm.

## The Two Tracks

The policy answers two different questions, and every clause belongs to exactly one.

The **violation track** asks whether someone broke the duty.

The **license track** asks whether a response was permitted.

One index rule governs both tracks. Duty and license verdicts on a person's act use that person's position. That position holds the facts, the actor's intent or carelessness, and what a reasonable person there would perceive. Other people's acts, words, expectations, and consents enter that judgment as facts. No one borrows a reasonableness standard from another person's position. A definition that names its own position overrides this default. Degree terms, such as grave, minor, credible, and substantial, are judged by the reasonable person in the acting person's position.

The tracks can diverge. A licensed response can coexist with no violation by anyone. When two people act on reasonable misperception, the tragedy has victims and no violator.

## Definitions

**Harm.** A setback to a person's body, mind, property, or rightful liberty. Harm to the mind means inflicted psychological injury. Hurt feelings alone do not qualify. Conveying information in good faith, believed true on reasonable grounds, is not infliction of psychological injury. Offense, disagreement, and losing a fair contest freely entered are not harm.

**Intent.** Acting with the aim of causing harm, or knowing harm is practically certain to follow.

**Careless risk.** Risk a reasonable person would not impose, given the activity's value and the precautions available.

**Consent.** Permission for a harm, given by the competent person who would bear it. Valid consent is informed, freely given, and revocable until the act passes its point of no return. Consent valid at that point covers what follows. Permission obtained by deception or coercion is not consent. A competent person's free, informed choice to bear a harm counts as valid consent to it. No one can consent to harm borne by someone else, with one exception. A guardian the adopting framework recognizes may consent for a person lacking competence, in that person's interest. Where consent cannot be obtained in time, consent is presumed for harm a reasonable person would accept to avert greater harm to the same person. Consent is never presumed against a competent refusal the actor knows of or should know of. Deliberately avoiding that knowledge counts as knowing. A guardian's refusal binds only within the person's interest. It never defeats presumed consent for harm needed to avert grave harm to that person. A guardian's refusal outside the person's interest leaves consent unobtainable, so the presumption may still operate.

**Permitted harm.** Harm within valid consent, harm within a licensed response under the Rules, or harm imposed by lawful institutions through fair process. All other harm is unpermitted.

**Attempt.** Taking a substantial step toward inflicting harm the agent intends, knowing or having reason to know the facts that make it unpermitted.

**Threat.** Intentionally or recklessly leading another to expect unpermitted harm from the threatener, or from forces the threatener directs or controls. A threat can travel through words, conduct, or both. Anger, hyperbole, and jokes are no threat when a reasonable person in the recipient's position would find no serious commitment behind them.

**Danger.** A danger exists for a responder when a reasonable person in the responder's position would perceive qualifying harm. Qualifying harm is imminent, in progress, or reversible on the spot. It befalls at least one person, whether or not yet identified, the endangered. The endangered may be the responder or another. It is neither within valid consent nor imposed by lawful institutions through fair process. Harm the responder knows or should know lies within a licensed response is not qualifying harm. Harm to the responder's own body or mind stays qualifying even then. The source's state of mind does not matter. Intent, carelessness, accident, and innocent conduct can each pose a danger. Attempted concealment does not defeat a danger the responder can perceive. A danger is live while the harm can still be averted or undone on the spot.

**Source.** The person or object whose presence, condition, conduct, or motion carries or sustains a danger.

**Torture.** Deliberately inflicting severe suffering to break a person's will.

## Rules

The violation rules judge acts. The license rules judge responses. The index rule governs both.

### Rule V1: The duty

Do not inflict on another person harm you intend, knowing or having reason to know the facts that make it unpermitted. Inflicting includes acting through an instrument, an innocent agent, or a person one coerces. Do not attempt such harm. Do not threaten harm, knowing or having reason to know the facts that make the threatened harm unpermitted. Do not impose careless risk of harm on others. Do not torture. Neither consent, nor license, nor institutional process permits torture or the threat of it. These duties bind everyone, always, with only the boundaries these rules state.

### Rule V2: Consent

Harm within valid consent violates no duty, except torture, which nothing permits. Surgery and sport live here.

### Rule V3: Institutions

Harm imposed by lawful institutions through fair process violates no duty. Fair process means the institution's own lawful procedures, as the adopting framework recognizes them.

### Rule V4: Staging

Deliberately or recklessly creating the appearance of a danger, without reasonable precautions against misperception, imposes careless risk and violates the duty. A stager in violation bears the loss any licensed response the staged appearance foreseeably provokes, directly or through further reasonable misperception.

### Rule V5: Good faith

These rules are written for good faith. Deliberately engineering a rule's conditions, to bring harm about or to excuse it, violates the duty. The letter of no rule launders a plan to hurt someone.

### Rule L1: The response license

A responder may answer a danger with harm, or with a threat of harm. The response is licensed only while all four conditions hold.

1. **Live danger.** A danger exists for the responder and is live.
2. **Necessity.** No course inflicting less harm and open to the responder averts the danger. If an exit avoids all threatened harm, take the exit.
3. **Proportionality.** The response may not greatly exceed the harm it prevents. Deadly force answers only credible dangers to life or limb. If every effective response is excessive, no harmful response is licensed.
4. **Clean hands.** The responder did not bring the danger about by conduct violating the duty, or by deliberate provocation. Deliberate provocation means conduct meant to draw an attack. Whether the responder brought the danger about turns only on their conduct before it arose. A danger arises anew once the responder has fully withdrawn and shown it. It also arises anew when a response that held a license exceeds it. A response that was never licensed triggers a new arising only when it greatly exceeds what the responder's conduct foreseeably invited. Conduct before a new arising no longer counts as bringing the new danger about. Clean hands never bars answering a danger the responder's own conduct created, when the response protects someone else. That responder owes repair for every harm the response inflicts.

A licensed response may fall only on the source of the danger, except as Rule L2 permits. Harm to a property interest in the source counts as falling on the source. Where a person wrongfully made the source carry the danger, that person owes repair to the innocent source or its owner. Where the property's own behavior carried the danger, and no person wrongfully made it do so, no repair is owed. Otherwise the responder owes the blameless owner repair. A response in threat form is licensed while all four conditions hold. Its condition-three measure is the harm the threat itself inflicts, never the harm it names. A response in threat form may name harm to the source alone, never to anyone else. A warning naming only what a license would permit is no threat and needs no license. A licensed threat violates no duty. Carrying it out is a fresh response, judged on its own conditions. A threat of deadly force stays non-deadly while the responder's aim stops at deterrence. Neither torture nor the threat of it is ever licensed.

### Rule L2: Bystanders

Answering or escaping a grave danger may harm a bystander's property when conditions one through four hold. It may never harm a bystander's body or mind. The property harm must stay minor beside the harm answered or escaped. The responder owes the bystander repair.

### Rule L3: Expiry

The license ends the moment the danger stops being live. Harm after that point is retaliation and violates the duty. The aftermath belongs to lawful institutions. Private payback stays forbidden.

### Rule L4: No transfer

The license attaches to the live danger alone. It never extends to grievance, group membership, or past wrongs. Defense of the endangered is direct response, never transfer. Defense of a person fails against a response licensed by that person's own duty-violating conduct. This bar binds only responders who know or should know those facts.

## Dependencies

Seven terms take their content from the adopting framework: person, property, rightful liberty, fair contest, lawful institutions, fair process, and competence. Who may serve as guardian follows the competence account. This policy does not define these terms. State your account of each when you adopt it. The lawful institutions and fair process delegation carries heavy loads, including arrest and other pre-judgment coercion. The reasonable person is the policy's own standard and is not delegated.

## Judgment and Law

This policy is a personal decision procedure. It is neither a complete moral system nor a substitute for law.

It does not excuse violations of valid law, with one exception. A licensed response to a grave and live danger needs no statute's permission, and no statute removes it. Outside that exception, when valid law forbids conduct this policy permits, follow the law. When valid law permits conduct this policy forbids, follow this policy. When valid law requires conduct this policy forbids, seek lawful review, exception, or accommodation where available. Unjust law is challenged through institutional process, never answered with private force. The policy cannot settle every conflict of conscience. Valid means valid under the adopting framework's foundations, never merely enacted.

Where the text is silent or indeterminate, choose the least harmful reasonable course. Interpret doubtful provisions according to the policy's spirit: avoid unnecessary harm, use force only against live danger, and repair avoidable losses.

## Amendment

Reopen this policy only when one of three things happens.

1. Two provisions give conflicting guidance on the same case.
2. A recurring real situation defeats the policy's stated spirit.
3. An ambiguity materially changes how a conscientious person would behave.

A newly constructed hypothetical reopens nothing. The reasonable person, the least-harm fallback, and the controlling spirit exist because enumeration must stop.

## Lineage

This policy is a specification of nonmaleficence, the duty not to inflict harm on others. Nonmaleficence entered modern ethics as one of W. D. Ross's prima facie duties (Skelton, 2022). The definition of harm adapts Joel Feinberg's account of harm as a setback to interests (Feinberg, 1984). The consent definition follows Feinberg's account of consent as the bearer's own normative power, and the floor takes his anti-paternalist line (Feinberg, 1986). The response conditions draw on Jeff McMahan's account of liability to defensive harm (McMahan, 2009). The separation of necessity from proportionality follows the standard analysis of self-defense (Frowe & Parry, 2021). The license against innocent sources extends Judith Jarvis Thomson's account of self-defense (Thomson, 1991). The threat definition borrows from the true threat doctrine in American law. Hyperbole is no threat (_Watts v. United States_, 1969). A speaker must intend the threat or be reckless about how it lands (_Counterman v. Colorado_, 2023). The two-track structure states the distinction between wrongdoing and permission as explicit rules.
