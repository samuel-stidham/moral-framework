# The Harm Boundary - Version 1.1.0

## Purpose

This document states one rule about harm, with every condition explicit. It is written in a neutral voice so anyone may adopt it. It stands alone and requires no surrounding framework. The goal is a rule with boundaries rather than exceptions. Nothing here depends on a hidden adjective. Any framework may adopt this policy by referencing it.

## Definitions

**Harm.** A setback to a person's body, mind, property, or rightful liberty. Harm to the mind means inflicted psychological injury, and hurt feelings alone do not qualify. Offense, disagreement, and losing a fair contest freely entered are not harm.

**Intent.** Acting with the aim of causing harm, or knowing harm is practically certain to follow.

**Careless risk.** Risk a reasonable person would not impose, given the activity's value and the precautions available.

**Threat.** A credible expression of serious intent to inflict imminent harm. Credibility is judged from the target's position, by what a reasonable person there would perceive. Anger, hyperbole, and jokes are no threat when a reasonable listener would hear no serious intent. A threat is live while it can still be averted.

**Initiation.** The first intentional infliction of harm, or the first live threat of it, against a person posing no threat. The medium does not matter. A spoken threat and a fraudulent promise initiate as surely as a fist. Whoever provokes a confrontation intending to draw an attack counts as its initiator. Offense and disagreement never count as initiation.

**Consent.** Permission for harm given by a competent person, informed, freely given, and revocable. Permission obtained by deception or coercion is not consent.

## Rules

**Rule 1: The duty.** Do not initiate harm. Do not impose careless risk of harm on others. This duty binds always. It has the boundaries stated in these rules and no exceptions beyond them.

**Rule 2: Consent.** Harm accepted through valid consent does not violate the duty. Surgery and sport live here.

**Rule 3: Defense.** Responsive harm is permitted only while all three conditions hold.

1. The threat is live. Harm that can no longer avert anything is retaliation and stays forbidden.
2. The harm is necessary. If a harmless exit exists, take the exit.
3. The harm is proportionate. It may rise only to what stopping the threat requires.

These conditions cover defense of self and defense of others equally. Defense keys on the reasonable perception of a threat, never on the attacker's hidden state of mind. Whoever recklessly stages the appearance of a deadly threat bears that risk under Rule 1.

**Rule 4: Expiry.** The license to respond ends the moment the threat ends. What follows belongs to lawful institutions. Private payback stays forbidden.

**Rule 5: No transfer.** The license attaches to the live threat alone. It never extends to grievance, group membership, or past wrongs.

## Dependencies

Four terms take their content from the adopting framework: property, rightful liberty, fair contest, and lawful institutions. This policy does not define them. State your account of each when you adopt it.

## Lineage

This policy is a specification of nonmaleficence, the duty not to inflict harm on others. Nonmaleficence entered modern ethics as one of W. D. Ross's prima facie duties (Skelton, 2022). The definition of harm adapts Joel Feinberg's account of harm as a setback to interests (Feinberg, 1984). The defense conditions follow Jeff McMahan's account of liability to defensive harm (McMahan, 2009). The threat definition follows the true threat doctrine in American law. Angry hyperbole is no threat (_Watts v. United States_, 1969). A speaker must intend the threat or be reckless about how it lands (_Counterman v. Colorado_, 2023). The policy states these ideas as explicit conditions so the rule needs no external exceptions.
